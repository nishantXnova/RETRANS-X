Honest context. Stream's value proposition is O(n) — flat ~228K tok/s across all sequence lengths (forward-only benchmark), O(n) memory (6.77 GB at T=65,536 on a T4), and a projected crossover vs. quadratic attention at T≈9k (1.73× at T=16k, forward-only). The naive JIT scan measures Python-loop overhead, not asymptotics; the current GPU implementation is the fused Triton scan, plus a chunked two-level scan for low-B·H, occupancy-starved shapes and a shape-conditional auto-dispatch rule (chunked iff B·H≤128, else fused; no T threshold) — all benchmarked on T4 (see §5.9 of the report). The kernel body is still a serial loop over T (grid B·H), not a true O(log T) parallel tree scan. End-to-end training throughput is being re-measured after fixing a missing torch.cuda.synchronize(); earlier "12 ms/iter / 73×" figures were CPU-scheduling artifacts. The transformer still wins on loss at matched small-scale parameter counts — this diagram is the architecture, not the final verdict.
